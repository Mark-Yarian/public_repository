# Conceptual Compression and Context Fidelity in Human–LLM Collaboration

Humans often communicate complex ideas through a few conceptual landmarks while omitting relationships that are familiar to them but unavailable to a listener. Large language models (LLMs) can reconstruct missing relationships, but may also introduce plausible, unsupported inferences. This paper proposes that unapproved conversational drift may depend less on compressed communication itself than on unverified model inferences being promoted into accepted context during subsequent turns. It distinguishes biological conceptual compression from LLM reconstruction, recoverable omissions from genuinely unavailable information, and authorized goal changes from unapproved drift. An intent-graph representation and proposed measures of task-weighted distortion and context debt are introduced, together with five falsifiable hypotheses and a controlled evaluation design. The causal mechanism remains a research hypothesis, not a demonstrated finding.

**Evidence:** Theoretical synthesis, falsifiable hypotheses, and proposed controlled study; no original empirical results.

**Review status:** Author-released working paper; not peer reviewed.
