# Baseline Is Not Health

Condition-monitoring systems commonly compare current measurements with baselines, thresholds, learned distributions, or reference windows. This paper argues that a stable initial distribution is evidence of repeatability, not evidence of health, and proposes explicit separation between observed and health-validated baselines together with intervention-bounded temporal epochs. Maintenance events become evidence-bearing boundaries for pre/post comparison without automatic causal attribution. The architecture preserves provenance, timing uncertainty, operating context, and historical baselines so later evidence can reinterpret earlier stable states without rewriting history.

**Evidence:** Conceptual architecture and proposed baseline/maintenance evaluation; no measured diagnostic improvement.

**Review status:** Author-released working paper; not peer reviewed.
