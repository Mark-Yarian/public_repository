# Human–Machine Research: Interpretability and Decision Support

**Public research area · Conceptual overview · Not a clinical or industrial deployment claim**

Human–machine research studies how people understand, supervise, and collaborate with technical systems. A useful interface should help someone decide **what is known, what is inferred, and which action still requires human authority**.

## Questions worth investigating
- How should a system distinguish direct observation from its interpretation?
- When does a visualization clarify evidence, and when might it make uncertainty harder to notice?
- What does a human reviewer need to challenge or reverse an automated recommendation?
- How can people with different levels of technical training understand the same result?

## Useful vocabulary
| Term | Plain-language meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Human-in-the-loop** | A process that requires human participation at a defined decision point. |
| **Decision support** | Information or recommendations intended to assist, not silently replace, a person's judgment. |
| **Interpretability** | How understandable a system's behavior or output is to its users. |
| **Audit trail** | A traceable account of relevant actions, observations, and decisions. |
| **Automation bias** | The tendency to trust an automated suggestion more than the evidence warrants. |

## Reading this area
The questions here concern broadly established research concepts. They do not imply that any SynapticSteel product has independently proven safety, autonomy, reliability, or suitability for a particular environment. All public claims require their own evidence and release review.
