# Experimental Methods: Claims, Controls, and Replication

**Public research area · Conceptual overview · Not an experiment report**

A useful experiment turns a question into a test that another person could critique or repeat. Strong scientific communication distinguishes a plausible explanation from a measurement, a measurement from a causal claim, and a first observation from a reproducible result.

## Questions worth investigating
- What would count as evidence **against** the hypothesis?
- Which baseline or control would reveal a simpler explanation?
- How do measurement error and missing observations affect a conclusion?
- Which parts of a reported result are reproducible by someone outside the project?

## Useful vocabulary
| Term | Plain-language meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Hypothesis** | An explanation or prediction that can be tested. |
| **Baseline** | A reference result used to interpret a new method. |
| **Control** | A condition designed to isolate what caused an outcome. |
| **Replication** | Repeating a study to test whether its result holds up. |
| **Uncertainty** | The range of plausible interpretations or values supported by the evidence. |

## Reading this area
Research plans, simulations, demonstrations, and real measurements have different evidentiary value. This public library does not present unpublished experiments as results, and it does not expose private datasets, model parameters, or original methods to make a conceptual overview look more impressive.
