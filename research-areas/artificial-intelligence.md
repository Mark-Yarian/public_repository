# Artificial Intelligence: Evaluation and Reliability

**Public research area · Conceptual overview · Not a scientific result**

Artificial intelligence includes systems that recognize patterns, generate language, make predictions, and support decisions. Useful research asks not only whether a model produces a convincing answer, but also **when it fails, how the failure is detected, and what evidence supports its output**.

## Questions worth investigating
- How should an AI system communicate uncertainty and limitations?
- What makes an evaluation meaningful outside a controlled benchmark?
- How can we distinguish observed improvement from a change in test conditions?
- When should an automated recommendation require human review?

## Useful vocabulary
| Term | Plain-language meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Inference** | Using a trained model to produce an output from new input. |
| **Evaluation** | Testing behavior against explicit criteria and known cases. |
| **Generalization** | Performing adequately on conditions not identical to training examples. |
| **Distribution shift** | A change between the conditions a system learned from and the conditions it encounters. |
| **Calibration** | How well a stated confidence corresponds to real-world correctness. |

## Reading this area
These are general research questions and public-domain concepts, **not claims about SynapticSteel models, proprietary methods, consciousness, or validated system performance**. An experiment requires a defined method, a comparison or baseline, and reported limitations. No paper from this area has been released through this repository as of its current `INDEX_ONLY` state.
