# RA-024 — Formal simulations and physical explanations

**Research article · Version 0.1 · Published 2026-11-11 (America/New_York) · Not peer reviewed**

**Type:** Formal-model methods review. **Review stage:** Initial critical synthesis. **Data status:** No new empirical dataset or result; proposed comparisons are unrun.

## Research question and argument

A symbolic system can be internally well defined without describing the physical world. A research proposal should state its objects, operations, information access, transition rules, and predicted observations. Replacing a physical noun with a software metaphor does not supply that mapping.

## Evidence and limits

[Faux and Gates, 2004](REFERENCES.md#adinkras) presents graphical tools for supersymmetric representation theory. [LLVM project](REFERENCES.md#llvm) specifies a computational representation. Neither supports the claim that manipulating a diagram creates physical reality. Likewise, an existence statement alone does not provide an executable selection procedure.

## Proposed comparison

1. Define a finite model or clearly specified state space, its transition rules, and what its observer can access.
2. Separate properties assumed by construction from outcomes derived from the rules.
3. Compare proposed selection and update procedures with simpler explicit algorithms.
4. If a physical interpretation is proposed, state units, a measurement mapping, and an observation that could contradict it.
5. Track symbolic replay separately from the external sequence of actual observations.

## Interpretation and failure criteria

An interesting simulation can remain useful without a cosmological interpretation. Without a testable measurement mapping, a statement about hidden universal code remains speculative. This standalone article is not cited as evidence for a released STP mechanism.

**Vocabulary — Falsification criterion: a specified observation that would count against a proposed explanation.**
