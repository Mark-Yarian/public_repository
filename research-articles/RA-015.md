# RA-015 — Multirate computation and temporal evidence

**Research article · Version 0.2 · Expanded 2026-11-13 (America/New_York) · Not peer reviewed**

**Type:** Research synthesis and methods proposal. **Review stage:** Initial critical synthesis. **Data status:** No new empirical dataset or result; proposed comparisons are unrun.

## Research question and argument

A fast event lane, a slow summary lane, and an asynchronous controller can interact without sharing a reliable physical event time. A useful multirate proposal must specify what each update observes, how stale state is treated, and which temporal relation matters to the task.

## Evidence and limits

[Davies et al., 2018](REFERENCES.md#loihi) documents a particular spiking processor and workload results. It motivates concrete event-driven comparisons but does not prove that named brainwave lanes or a wavefront schedule improve every application.

## Proposed comparison

1. Use one trace with independently known event timing for a single-rate controller, an ordinary hierarchy, and the proposed multirate design.
2. Vary delay, reordering, sampling gaps, clock offset, and update cadence independently.
3. Record the support interval and uncertainty of each observation and derived summary.
4. Measure task error, false temporal joins, state staleness, deadline misses, and complete cost.
5. Repeat with ordinary event-queue routing before attributing an effect to spike encoding.

## Data needed to assess the claim

| Record | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Physical event support | What time interval the observation represents |
| Clock and uncertainty | Origin, domain, and uncertainty bounds |
| Update state | Which revision each lane consumed |
| Transport history | Delay, reordering, and discarded events |
| Task outcome | Quality, latency, and energy boundary |

## Interpretation and failure criteria

A synchronized animation or a biological frequency label is insufficient temporal evidence. A scheduling benefit would support the tested task and configuration, not a general consciousness mechanism. No timing or energy benchmark accompanies this article.

**Vocabulary — Multirate system: interacting components that update at different intervals.**

## Historical cluster case study and proposed experiment

**Evidence status (2026-11-13):** This section summarizes preserved historical source and JSON observations, not a new controlled experiment. Reported counters are retrospective system records, not independently reproduced experimental results. The [historical evidence ledger](HISTORICAL_CLUSTER_EVIDENCE.md) gives the shared provenance and publication limits.

The historical temporal report documented 60 mapped features but had periodic historian scheduling disabled. Glial Equilibrium tracked a six-hour diagnostic window and separated local-cycle lag changes from whole-system backlog balance. These are documented clock and aggregation distinctions rather than evidence of a successful multirate predictor.

**Test:** Compare fixed-rate capture with event-change-aware sampling under a matched observation budget, injected jitter and missing data. Preserve acquisition, source-event, model-processing and report times separately. Score missed transitions, forecasting accuracy, failure latency and sampling cost.
