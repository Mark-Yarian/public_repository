# RA-022 — Curiosity as bounded exploration and transfer

**Research article · Version 0.1 · Published 2026-11-11 (America/New_York) · Not peer reviewed**

**Type:** Research synthesis and methods proposal. **Review stage:** Initial critical synthesis. **Data status:** No new empirical dataset or result; proposed comparisons are unrun.

## Research question and argument

Curiosity can be investigated through the information a system seeks, the cost it incurs, and what it later retains or transfers. Novel output alone is an inadequate success criterion. A developmental or dream-like metaphor can suggest variables but cannot supply a maturity scale or an empirical result.

## Evidence and limits

[Ofria and Wilke, 2004](REFERENCES.md#avida) and [Evci et al., 2020](REFERENCES.md#rigl) offer concrete examples of experimentally specified variation and adaptation. They motivate controlled baselines, not a claim that exploration in a new system has a biological experience or a proven general benefit.

## Proposed comparison

1. Define novelty, task correctness, retention, transfer, and the allowed exploration budget.
2. Compare a fixed task policy, random exploration, novelty-directed exploration, and a simple uncertainty rule.
3. Keep task and resource exposure comparable and score on a separate held-out task set.
4. Ablate external memory, offline replay, and structural adaptation before evaluating a combined cycle.
5. Preserve all attempted explorations and report constraint violations and unproductive novelty.

## Data needed to assess the claim

| Record | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Exploration opportunity | What the system could choose to inspect |
| Selection rule | Why an option was chosen |
| Complete cost | Time, compute, data, and action expenditure |
| Retained knowledge | What survived and was later used |
| Transfer outcome | Useful behavior on a distinct task population |

## Interpretation and failure criteria

Reject a curiosity advantage that consists only of generating unusual material or receiving more attempts. Useful exploration should improve a specified later task within its budget. No biological dream or maturity mechanism is validated here.

**Vocabulary — Transfer: useful performance on a task or condition different from the one used to learn or select the method.**
