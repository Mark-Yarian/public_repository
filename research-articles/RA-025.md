# RA-025 — Thorium claims and fuel-cycle comparison boundaries

**Research article · Version 0.1 · Published 2026-11-11 (America/New_York) · Not peer reviewed**

**Type:** Scientific background and literature-comparison review. **Review stage:** Initial critical synthesis. **Data status:** No new empirical dataset or result; proposed comparisons are unrun.

## Research question and argument

A claim about thorium needs a specified fuel cycle, reactor concept, comparison system, and accounting boundary. An element name cannot establish a universal safety, waste, or resource advantage. This standalone review organizes the evidence needed for a literature comparison rather than proposing a reactor design.

## Evidence and limits

[IAEA, 2022](REFERENCES.md#thorium) discusses deployment options and the requirements of thorium-based nuclear energy. Thorium-232 is fertile rather than directly fissile; a fuel cycle needs a fissile component. Those distinctions do not establish the performance of every design that uses thorium.

## Proposed comparison

1. Identify the exact reactor and fuel-cycle assumptions in each cited study.
2. Separate conversion, fuel fabrication, operation, reprocessing where assumed, and waste management boundaries.
3. Compare specified alternatives using the same outcome definitions and accounting periods.
4. Record missing evidence and uncertainty rather than completing an unfinished historical discussion with assumed results.
5. Keep literature findings, prospective designs, and demonstrated operating evidence in separate fields.

## Data needed to assess the claim

| Record | Purpose |

## Interpretation and failure criteria

A favorable result for one design is not a blanket property of thorium. The cited technical report supports a bounded background review; no construction procedure, new fuel-cycle result, or operating claim is added. No direct association with a released STP paper is asserted.

**Vocabulary — Fertile material: material that can be converted into fissile material through nuclear processes.**
