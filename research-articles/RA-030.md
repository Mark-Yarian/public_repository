# RA-030 — Radiation exposure variables and tissue-response evidence

**Research article · Version 0.1 · Published 2026-11-11 (America/New_York) · Not peer reviewed**

**Type:** Biophysical background and evidence-review method. **Review stage:** Initial critical synthesis. **Data status:** No new empirical dataset or result; proposed comparisons are unrun.

## Research question and argument

A radiation-injury literature review should preserve the exposure variables that make observations comparable. Radiation type, absorbed dose, dose rate, exposure geometry, tissue, and observation time cannot be collapsed into a single dramatic label.

## Evidence and limits

[CDC, 2024](REFERENCES.md#radiation) describes acute radiation syndrome in the context of substantial penetrating exposure to most or all of the body over a short period. Its clinical guidance is distinct from this research-background article. This review does not supply patient-specific interpretation or a treatment protocol.

## Proposed comparison

1. Build a literature evidence table using original study or guidance definitions, without conducting exposure experiments.
2. Record dose quantity and units, radiation type, dose rate, exposed region, and uncertainty.
3. Separate whole-body, partial-body, localized tissue, and internal-contamination descriptions where the cited evidence distinguishes them.
4. Preserve the time of exposure, observation, and outcome assessment in each record.
5. Keep mechanistic descriptions, population observations, and clinical recommendations in separate evidence fields.

## Data needed to assess the claim

| Record | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Exposure definition | Radiation type, quantity, units, and geometry |
| Temporal support | Exposure duration and observation interval |
| Biological context | Tissue, organism/population, and stated conditions |
| Outcome definition | The response actually measured or described |
| Evidence and uncertainty | Original source, limitations, and interpretation boundary |

## Interpretation and failure criteria

Do not transfer a reported effect to a different exposure condition without an explicit evidential argument. This is a literature-organization method with no new biological measurement, clinical dataset, or care recommendation. It has no direct released-paper association in this edition.

**Vocabulary — Dose rate: a stated radiation dose quantity accumulated per unit time.**
