# RA-002 — Peer discovery, leader election, and recovery

**Research article · Version 0.1 · Published 2026-11-11 (America/New_York) · Not peer reviewed**

**Type:** Research synthesis and methods proposal. **Review stage:** Initial critical synthesis. **Data status:** No new empirical dataset or result; proposed comparisons are unrun.

## Research question and argument

Finding a reachable peer answers a discovery question. Selecting a coordinator, maintaining a consistent decision history, and recovering after a partition are additional questions. A connection-count heuristic can be evaluated as a placement rule, but should not inherit consensus guarantees merely because one participant is called the server.

## Evidence and limits

[Ongaro and Ousterhout, 2014](REFERENCES.md#raft) separates election, replicated-log behavior, and safety assumptions. [ZeroMQ project](REFERENCES.md#zeromq) documents distinct messaging patterns. Ordinary REQ/REP requires alternating request and reply; a nonblocking operation does not by itself provide a complete asynchronous retry or recovery protocol.

## Proposed comparison

1. State membership, failure assumptions, ordering requirements, and the meaning of an accepted decision.
2. Compare a discovery-only heuristic, a simple coordinator, and an explicitly specified replicated decision protocol.
3. Inject asymmetric reachability, partition, delayed replies, restart, and duplicate requests using the same workload.
4. Track decisions that conflict, repeat, disappear, or remain indeterminate after connectivity returns.
5. Keep coordination success separate from permission to execute a requested operation.

## Data needed to assess the claim

| Record | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Membership and epoch | Which participants and decision generation applied |
| Request identity | Whether a retry repeats an existing operation |
| Acceptance evidence | Which rule admitted a decision |
| Failure schedule | What became unreachable and when |
| Recovery outcome | Time to valid progress, stale work, and unresolved state |

## Interpretation and failure criteria

Passing a reachability test does not establish consistent leadership. Evaluate liveness and decision integrity separately. This is a proposed comparison of public protocol concepts, not a description of a currently deployed cluster.

**Vocabulary — Epoch: an identified generation of state used to distinguish predecessor work from current work.**
