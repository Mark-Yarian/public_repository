# RA-014 — Biological inspiration as a computational comparison

**Research article · Version 0.1 · Published 2026-11-11 (America/New_York) · Not peer reviewed**

**Type:** Research synthesis and methods proposal. **Review stage:** Initial critical synthesis. **Data status:** No new empirical dataset or result; proposed comparisons are unrun.

## Research question and argument

Biological inspiration becomes useful engineering when a proposed correspondence identifies its variables, scale, computation, and predicted difference from a simpler model. A dendritic tree, a messaging network, and a branching data structure may resemble one another while implementing different dynamics.

## Evidence and limits

[Gidon et al., 2020](REFERENCES.md#dendrites) reports particular nonlinear computations in human cortical dendrites. [Kagan et al., 2022](REFERENCES.md#dishbrain) reports a separate closed-loop culture experiment. Neither establishes that a tree-shaped artificial network, a copied cell label, or a software maintenance schedule has the same mechanism or subjective experience.

## Proposed comparison

1. Choose one claimed correspondence and define the biological observation scale and computational operation.
2. Compare point-like and compartmental or graded models under stated capacity and data constraints.
3. Separate effects of morphology, active dynamics, learning rule, and feedback contingency.
4. Use a held-out prediction or control task and report model complexity and complete resource cost.
5. Keep biological measurements, engineering hypotheses, and consciousness interpretation in separate result fields.

## Data needed to assess the claim

| Record | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Observation scale | Cell, circuit, behavior, or whole-system claim |
| Mapped operation | Input, internal transformation, and output |
| Comparison models | Simpler and proposed alternatives |
| Controlled factors | Capacity, data, feedback, and learning rule |
| Outcome | Predictive value, error, complexity, and cost |

## Interpretation and failure criteria

Reject an architectural claim that depends only on visual resemblance or a borrowed biological term. Do not infer fixed brain-wide cell proportions or universal cognitive mechanisms from one preparation. This article proposes comparisons rather than a theory of the whole brain.
