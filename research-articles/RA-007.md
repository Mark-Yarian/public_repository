# RA-007 — Swarm coordination under partial observations

**Research article · Version 0.1 · Published 2026-11-11 (America/New_York) · Not peer reviewed**

**Type:** Research synthesis and methods proposal. **Review stage:** Initial critical synthesis. **Data status:** No new empirical dataset or result; proposed comparisons are unrun.

## Research question and argument

A swarm proposal should state its task, local information, communication rules, and success criterion. The number of participants or the appearance of collective motion cannot establish useful coordination. Extra participants also introduce communication, contention, and correlated-failure opportunities.

## Evidence and limits

[ZeroMQ project](REFERENCES.md#zeromq) specifies messaging patterns rather than task-level intelligence. [Ongaro and Ousterhout, 2014](REFERENCES.md#raft) addresses a particular consistency problem under stated assumptions. Their existence does not imply that an arbitrary network of models is optimal, fault tolerant, or conscious.

## Proposed comparison

1. Compare independent agents, a simple coordinator, and the proposed local-interaction policy.
2. Hold total task budget and admissible information fixed across comparison arms.
3. Vary topology, bandwidth, delay, participant failure, and correlated bad observations separately.
4. Measure task quality, completion rate, communication volume, convergence, and recovery to valid progress.
5. Evaluate new environments and random seeds; retain failed and nonconvergent runs.

## Data needed to assess the claim

| Record | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Local information | What each participant could observe |
| Message ancestry | Content, sender revision, delivery, and consumption |
| Shared causes | Inputs or transforms common to multiple agents |
| Complete cost | Compute, communication, waiting, and retries |
| Task outcome | Quality, latency, failure, and abstention |

## Interpretation and failure criteria

A coordination benefit must exceed a matched simpler baseline and survive transport failures. Agreement among dependent participants is insufficient corroboration. Aggregate CPU counts or clock rates do not measure the usable throughput of the swarm.

**Vocabulary — Stigmergy: coordination through changes in a shared environment rather than direct instructions alone.**
