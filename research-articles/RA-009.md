# RA-009 — Failure memory from logs to later decisions

**Research article · Version 0.1 · Published 2026-11-11 (America/New_York) · Not peer reviewed**

**Type:** Research synthesis and methods proposal. **Review stage:** Initial critical synthesis. **Data status:** No new empirical dataset or result; proposed comparisons are unrun.

## Research question and argument

A log-analysis system can detect patterns without demonstrating that it learns usefully from failure. The question is whether retained failure evidence changes a later decision on a comparable, previously unseen case. A persuasive retrospective explanation is not an independent outcome measurement.

## Evidence and limits

[Shojaee et al., 2025](REFERENCES.md#reasoning) reports model behavior in controlled puzzles. It motivates documenting task complexity and failure type, while leaving any benefit from a proposed external failure-memory system to a separate comparison. Historical implementation narratives are author reports unless their execution evidence is independently available.

## Proposed comparison

1. Define failure categories and comparable future opportunities before selecting examples.
2. Compare no cross-episode memory, unstructured retrieval, and outcome-linked failure memory.
3. Hold model revision, task difficulty, scoring, and execution budget fixed.
4. Test repeated-failure avoidance on held-out episodes and count newly introduced failures.
5. Measure useful task completion alongside abstention and total recovery cost.

## Data needed to assess the claim

| Record | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Failure episode | Intended action, consumed evidence, execution, and result |
| Recovery attempts | Successful and unsuccessful changes |
| Memory selection | What was retained and later retrieved |
| Recurrence opportunity | Exposure to a comparable future failure |
| Later outcome | Independent task score and changed decision |

## Interpretation and failure criteria

An alert, model response, or claimed historical execution does not by itself validate failure metabolism. This article does not reproduce operational timings or assert that a named system is currently working. The proposed claim is bounded to a controlled future comparison.

**Vocabulary — Recurrence rate: repeated failures divided by the relevant opportunities to fail again.**
