# RA-028 — Geomagnetic observations and reversal forecasts

**Research article · Version 0.1 · Published 2026-11-11 (America/New_York) · Not peer reviewed**

**Type:** Scientific background and uncertainty review. **Review stage:** Initial critical synthesis. **Data status:** No new empirical dataset or result; proposed comparisons are unrun.

## Research question and argument

A scientific discussion of the magnetic field should distinguish observed secular variation, excursions, reversals, and forecasts. A long interval since a past reversal does not by itself define a countdown or a predictable schedule.

## Evidence and limits

[USGS](REFERENCES.md#geomagnetism) describes polarity reversals as irregular, without apparent periodicity. This supports correcting an overdue-reversal interpretation; it does not supply a date for the next event or settle every practical consequence of field change.

## Proposed comparison

1. Name the phenomenon being measured and the observation series supporting it.
2. Separate direct measurements from reconstructed histories and prospective models.
3. Record temporal resolution, uncertainty, coverage, and any selection of notable intervals.
4. State the forecast target and baseline model before evaluating a prediction.
5. Keep space-weather events and engineering vulnerabilities distinct from a reversal forecast.

## Interpretation and failure criteria

An irregular event history does not justify a periodic countdown. This is a scientific-background review, not a preparedness directive or event prediction. No direct connection to an STP mechanism is asserted.

**Vocabulary — Secular variation: gradual change in the measured geomagnetic field over time.**
