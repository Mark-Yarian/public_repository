# RA-027 — Controllability in reference-conditioned generation

**Research article · Version 0.1 · Published 2026-11-11 (America/New_York) · Not peer reviewed**

**Type:** Research review and proposed evaluation. **Review stage:** Initial critical synthesis. **Data status:** No new empirical dataset or result; proposed comparisons are unrun.

## Research question and argument

A reference-conditioned image model should be evaluated for subject consistency, requested changes, and unintended carryover separately. A convincing demonstration does not establish perfect controllability across unfamiliar subjects, multiple references, or conflicting instructions.

## Evidence and limits

[Wu et al., 2025](REFERENCES.md#uno) reports a multi-image-conditioned generation approach and specific evaluations. It supplies a real research reference in place of placeholder project links. Its reported results do not guarantee that every output perfectly matches a user intention.

## Proposed comparison

1. Define subject-preservation and requested-change criteria before selecting outputs.
2. Compare prompt-only, single-reference, and multiple-reference conditions with a stated model revision.
3. Use held-out subjects and combinations, including conflicting attributes and irrelevant reference controls.
4. Score identity consistency, instruction fidelity, quality, and failures independently using a documented rubric.
5. Preserve every sampled output and its generation settings rather than presenting only successful examples.

## Data needed to assess the claim

| Record | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Reference identity | Exact input images and permitted transformations |
| Intent reference | Attributes to preserve and attributes to change |
| Generation revision | Model, prompt, settings, seed where controlled, and output |
| Evaluation population | All subjects, combinations, samples, and exclusions |
| Outcome | Consistency, instruction fidelity, quality, and failures |

## Interpretation and failure criteria

A stronger preservation score can coexist with poorer compliance with a requested change. Evaluate those outcomes separately and preserve the selection denominator. The relation to context fidelity is a proposed cross-modal comparison, not an established extension of the published theory.

**Vocabulary — Controllability: the extent to which specified inputs reliably produce specified changes in an output.**
