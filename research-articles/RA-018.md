# RA-018 — Context reconstruction and verified intent

**Research article · Version 0.1 · Published 2026-11-11 (America/New_York) · Not peer reviewed**

**Type:** Research synthesis and methods proposal. **Review stage:** Initial critical synthesis. **Data status:** No new empirical dataset or result; proposed comparisons are unrun.

## Research question and argument

People often express a task with a few landmarks and omit relationships familiar to them. A language model can supply plausible relationships, but plausibility does not establish that the user intended them. A research evaluation needs an independent record of intended constraints and authorized goal changes.

## Evidence and limits

The published SS-LAB-CC-001 paper proposes a context-fidelity study. [OpenAI](REFERENCES.md#openai) documents request/version considerations relevant to reproducibility; it does not validate a prompt-engineering method or guarantee deterministic behavior.

## Proposed comparison

1. Create full, recoverably compressed, and genuinely underspecified task versions with independently recorded intended relationships.
2. Compare unchecked reconstruction, an explicit inference ledger, and verification of selected gaps.
3. Keep user-approved changes separate from constraint losses and unsupported additions.
4. Use blinded scoring where feasible and test on held-out task families.
5. Record correction effort and downstream use of earlier guesses alongside final-answer quality.

## Data needed to assess the claim

| Record | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Intent reference | Required relationships and permitted changes |
| Context revision | Stated facts, guesses, accepted revisions, and unresolved gaps |
| Execution record | Model, complete request, and memory/retrieval state |
| Distortion score | Lost, altered, or unsupported task relationships |
| Correction cost | Effort and later consequences of repair |

## Interpretation and failure criteria

A polished answer may still misrepresent the task. Calling a prompt a program does not supply a formal specification, deterministic execution, or semantic correctness. Compare observable task outcomes rather than relying on a metaphor about the interface.

**Vocabulary — Context debt: unresolved or unsupported context that can impose later correction work.**
