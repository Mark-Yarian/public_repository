# RA-008 — Environmental feedback, confounding, and task fitness

**Research article · Version 0.1 · Published 2026-11-11 (America/New_York) · Not peer reviewed**

**Type:** Research synthesis and methods proposal. **Review stage:** Initial critical synthesis. **Data status:** No new empirical dataset or result; proposed comparisons are unrun.

## Research question and argument

An environment can carry queues, constraints, costs, memories, and feedback that affect agent behavior. Calling that environment a metabolism does not identify a mechanism by itself. The useful experiment separates environmental changes from changes in the model, memory, selection rule, and task population.

## Evidence and limits

[Ofria and Wilke, 2004](REFERENCES.md#avida) supplies methods for controlled computational evolution. [Evci et al., 2020](REFERENCES.md#rigl) supplies a concrete structural-adaptation comparison. Neither establishes that a proposed environmental layer is a necessary substrate for every adaptive or conscious artificial system.

## Proposed comparison

1. Define the environmental variables, available information, permitted actions, and fitness rule.
2. Compare fixed inference, external-memory change, policy change, and structural adaptation separately.
3. Ablate environmental cost, locality, feedback delay, and retained history one at a time.
4. Evaluate on held-out tasks after a controlled distribution shift and measure retention of earlier tasks.
5. Preserve the intervention schedule and any effect the observer has on the environment.

## Data needed to assess the claim

| Record | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Environment revision | Exactly which state and feedback changed |
| Adaptation revision | Model, memory, policy, or structure |
| Task population | Training exposure and held-out conditions |
| Cost and constraints | Compute, time, action limits, and violations |
| Outcome | Task quality, forgetting, recovery, and failure |

## Interpretation and failure criteria

A feedback loop can improve a selected score while losing useful behavior elsewhere. Reject a broad adaptation claim if its gains disappear under a fixed task distribution or matched cost. Biological necessity claims require independent evidence beyond the engineering analogy.

**Vocabulary — Confounding: a second changing factor that can explain an apparent effect.**
