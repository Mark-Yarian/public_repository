# RA-005 — Computational reproduction and bounded inheritance

**Research article · Version 0.1 · Published 2026-11-11 (America/New_York) · Not peer reviewed**

**Type:** Research synthesis and methods proposal. **Review stage:** Initial critical synthesis. **Data status:** No new empirical dataset or result; proposed comparisons are unrun.

## Research question and argument

A computational descendant needs a stated parent, an inherited-state boundary, and an independently testable continuity criterion. Copying files, migrating a process, adapting a model, and producing a separately executing descendant should be recorded as distinct operations. Their biological resemblance is a research motivation rather than an outcome label.

## Evidence and limits

[Ofria and Wilke, 2004](REFERENCES.md#avida) establishes an experimental setting for evolving, self-replicating programs. The published Digital Mitosis paper proposes a separate engineering framework. Evidence for the platform does not independently validate that proposed framework or a particular digital-cell implementation.

## Proposed comparison

1. Specify inherited executable configuration, memory, model state, identity records, limits, and excluded external state.
2. Compare a fresh installation, an ordinary clone, checkpoint migration, and a bounded-variation descendant.
3. Test independent restart, retained tasks, changed inputs, and corrupted inheritance partitions.
4. Measure behavioral continuity and novel task behavior separately using held-out tasks.
5. Preserve lineage and resource use for unsuccessful descendants as well as successful ones.

## Data needed to assess the claim

| Record | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Parent and descendant IDs | Explicit ancestry and independent execution identity |
| Inherited-state digest | What persisted and what was excluded |
| Variation budget | Operator, target, magnitude, and selection rule |
| Behavioral profile | Retained, lost, and changed task behavior |
| Population exposure | All eligible descendants, failures, and resource use |

## Interpretation and failure criteria

A restartable clone supports a bounded continuity claim, not a claim of biological life. An adaptation advantage must survive cloning controls, matched resources, and held-out evaluation. No reproduction trial is reported in this article.

**Vocabulary — Inheritance partition: the declared portion of a parent state transferred to a descendant.**
