# RA-029 — Selected event windows and missing opportunities

**Research article · Version 0.1 · Published 2026-11-11 (America/New_York) · Not peer reviewed**

**Type:** Selection-methods review. **Review stage:** Initial critical synthesis. **Data status:** No new empirical dataset or result; proposed comparisons are unrun.

## Research question and argument

A striking event selected after inspection can be useful for asking a question without establishing its frequency or cause. An anomaly study needs the complete set of eligible opportunities, the selection rule, missing coverage, and the family of comparisons considered.

## Evidence and limits

The released STP-008 manuscript supplies the selection-aware analytical background. A historical narrative without a primary dataset, locked event rule, and independent replication is not treated as a new empirical finding. No collective-consciousness effect is accepted as established in this article.

## Proposed comparison

1. Specify events, windows, exclusions, scoring, and comparison families before inspecting confirmatory outcomes.
2. Retain all eligible intervals and record unavailable observations separately from negative observations.
3. Account for overlapping windows, shared inputs, and other dependence among comparisons.
4. Evaluate exploratory patterns on a separate opportunity set with predefined controls.
5. Report all tested comparisons and unsuccessful replications alongside selected examples.

## Data needed to assess the claim

| Record | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Opportunity population | Every eligible event or interval |
| Selection mechanism | Why a case was retained, omitted, or highlighted |
| Coverage | Which observations were available |
| Comparison family | Signals, windows, outcomes, and repeated tests |
| Confirmation status | Exploratory pattern or independently evaluated hypothesis |

## Interpretation and failure criteria

A selected correlation cannot establish a physical cause or prevalence estimate by itself. Probability weighting also needs the assumptions stated in the parent manuscript. This article supplies a proposed audit of evidence selection, not a significance result or a new dataset.

**Vocabulary — Opportunity denominator: the complete set of relevant chances for an event or outcome to occur.**
