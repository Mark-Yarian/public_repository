# RA-016 — Useful throughput, memory, and communication costs

**Research article · Version 0.2 · Expanded 2026-11-13 (America/New_York) · Not peer reviewed**

**Type:** Research synthesis and methods proposal. **Review stage:** Initial critical synthesis. **Data status:** No new empirical dataset or result; proposed comparisons are unrun.

## Research question and argument

A hardware peak, token rate, clock rate, memory capacity, and completed-task rate measure different things. Comparisons need a workload, quality criterion, precision, concurrency, and accounting boundary. Dividing available memory by an assumed cell size does not establish a sustainable executing population.

## Evidence and limits

[Davies et al., 2018](REFERENCES.md#loihi), [Dettmers et al., 2022](REFERENCES.md#quantization), and [Peng et al., 2023](REFERENCES.md#rwkv) address different hardware, representation, and model settings. Their results should not be combined into one universal speed or memory claim. Unverified product metrics and promotional ratios are not adopted as measured results in this review.

## Proposed comparison

1. Define a useful completed task and the minimum accepted output quality.
2. State model and artifact revisions, precision, batch/concurrency, input/output lengths, and initialization policy.
3. Measure prefill or setup, processing, communication, decoding, idle time, and failures over the same interval.
4. Report peak and sustained memory, temporary state, queue growth, and population churn.
5. For specialized hardware or representations, include conversion and reconfiguration costs and disclose unsupported workloads.

## Interpretation and failure criteria

A faster narrow operation may leave whole-task cost unchanged or worse. Reject a scaling claim that omits communications, failed work, or quality differences. No current chip specification or vendor performance ranking is asserted here.

**Vocabulary — Throughput: completed units of a stated kind per unit time.**

## Historical cluster case study and proposed experiment

**Evidence status (2026-11-13):** This section summarizes preserved historical source and JSON observations, not a new controlled experiment. Reported counters are retrospective system records, not independently reproduced experimental results. The [historical evidence ledger](HISTORICAL_CLUSTER_EVIDENCE.md) gives the shared provenance and publication limits.

A preserved Cognitive Metabolism throughput record shows why task creation cannot be equated to useful throughput: it reported 66,407 tasks generated, 66,203 still queued, 1,032 failed dispatches, three accepted dispatches and zero completed model inferences at that checkpoint. This does not identify the sole cause or rule out work outside the interval.

**Test:** Replay identical inputs with unbounded task generation, queue caps, source deduplication and adaptive sampling. Measure quality-accepted completed tasks per wall-clock interval, end-to-end latency, total failed exposure and information retained. Queue growth alone is not an accomplishment.
