# RA-006 — Continuity through restart and long-running state

**Research article · Version 0.2 · Expanded 2026-11-12 (America/New_York) · Not peer reviewed**

**Type:** Research synthesis and methods proposal. **Review stage:** Initial critical synthesis. **Data status:** No new empirical dataset or result; proposed comparisons are unrun.

**Editorial provenance:** Version 0.2 teaching additions were prepared with AI assistance from the already released paper, notes and public terminology; the proposed framework and original article remain attributed to Mark Yarian.

## Research question and argument

Continuity is an observable relationship among states and executions, not a property established by elapsed uptime or conversational familiarity. Long-running recurrent state, persistent external memory, and an event history can preserve different information. A continuity experiment should specify which information and behavior must survive a boundary.

## Evidence and limits

[Peng et al., 2023](REFERENCES.md#rwkv) supplies a recurrent inference formulation for a sequence model; it does not guarantee perfect indefinite recall. [Ongaro and Ousterhout, 2014](REFERENCES.md#raft) illustrates why replicated decisions require explicit protocol state. These examples motivate separating model state, history, and external effects.

## Proposed comparison

1. Define restart, migration, copy, and replay boundaries together with the state each operation preserves.
2. Run matched histories with uninterrupted execution, cold restart, recorded-state restore, and missing-memory controls.
3. Inject delayed predecessor messages and partially completed external effects after a restart.
4. Compare retained task behavior, duplicated effects, and ability to explain results from the retained record.
5. Keep simulated time, event time, execution time, and historical retention as separately named variables.

## Interpretation and failure criteria

A coherent narrative can coexist with missing execution state. Reject a continuity claim whose required relation cannot be checked after reset. Historical duration and simulated time do not establish a change in physical time.

## Failure analysis: restore versus descent

A restored process and a newly identified descendant may begin with identical bytes. Record the operation that produced each one: a restart retains or reconstructs one running identity under a declared policy; a descendant introduces a separately tracked execution with an explicit parent relation. Test both against missing-memory and delayed-message cases. If either produces a duplicate external effect, matching memory bytes did not establish safe continuity.

The [lineage lab](../public-experiments/lineage-lab/README.md) shows only an in-memory parent/child relation. It has no durable checkpoint or external effect, so it cannot pass the restart and recovery experiments proposed in this article. [Lineage Memory on Yarian](https://yarian.com/terminology/lineage-memory) names one proposed category of inherited history; an actual result still needs preserved records and a test of their effect.

**Vocabulary — Checkpoint: a recorded state intended to support a specified restoration boundary.**
