# RA-020 — Reconstruction, identifiability, and physical source attribution

**Research article · Version 0.1 · Published 2026-11-11 (America/New_York) · Not peer reviewed**

**Type:** Analytical explainer and proposed evaluation. **Review stage:** Initial critical synthesis. **Data status:** No new empirical dataset or result; proposed comparisons are unrun.

## Research question and argument

A reconstruction can match an observed signal while leaving more than one explanation of its source. The scientific question is whether available measurements distinguish the competing explanations. The published STP-009 paper develops this issue for multi-machine vibration; the example below is an elementary explanation rather than a field result.

## Evidence and limits

Suppose two sources have the same measured transfer direction: y = a(x1 + x2). The assignments (x1, x2) = (1, 0) and (0, 1) produce the same y. Perfect reconstruction therefore leaves source identity ambiguous in this example. An additional observation helps only if it distinguishes the alternatives.

## Proposed comparison

1. State the source model, possible omitted sources, and the claim that must be identified.
2. Use independently referenced source operation and a documented timing/calibration record.
3. Compare calibration conditions with changed mounting, a withheld source, and an added interferer.
4. Measure incorrect attributions and justified abstentions as well as reconstruction error.
5. Preserve acquisition coverage and the complete tested condition set.

## Data needed to assess the claim

| Record | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Source reference | Independent evidence of which source operated |
| Transfer condition | Mounting, calibration, and model assumptions |
| Timing quality | Compatibility of witness and mixture observations |
| Attribution decision | Named source, supporting evidence, or abstention |
| Counterexample coverage | Alternative explanations actually challenged |

## Interpretation and failure criteria

A low residual alone cannot rule out an unmodeled contribution that the measurement model cannot distinguish. The numerical example establishes ambiguity only under its stated model. No new source-separation benchmark or industrial diagnosis is reported.

**Vocabulary — Identifiability: whether the available observations can distinguish the quantities or explanations being claimed.**
