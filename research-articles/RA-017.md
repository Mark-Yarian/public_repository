# RA-017 — Reasoning evaluations and failure taxonomy

**Research article · Version 0.1 · Published 2026-11-11 (America/New_York) · Not peer reviewed**

**Type:** Research synthesis and methods proposal. **Review stage:** Initial critical synthesis. **Data status:** No new empirical dataset or result; proposed comparisons are unrun.

## Research question and argument

A reasoning evaluation should expose what task was attempted, how difficulty was varied, what information was available, and what counted as failure. A broad statement that all reasoning is an illusion or that one external architecture solves it exceeds a bounded benchmark unless independently tested.

## Evidence and limits

[Shojaee et al., 2025](REFERENCES.md#reasoning) studies specified models in controlled puzzles and reports complexity-dependent behavior. It motivates careful benchmark design; its results do not settle every model, task, or proposed repair.

## Proposed comparison

1. Pin task, model, scoring, parser, budget, and benchmark revision.
2. Separate formatting failure, misunderstood rules, exact computation, search exhaustion, missing information, and tool failure.
3. Compare direct inference, supplied algorithms, retrieval, tools, and external memory under matched total budgets.
4. Use held-out instances and preserve the complete task denominator, including exclusions and failed runs.
5. Measure correctness, completion, calibration, abstention, and total resource use together.

## Data needed to assess the claim

| Record | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Task population | Families, difficulty, and generation procedure |
| Available information | Prompt, tools, memory, and supplied algorithms |
| Failure type | Which stage failed and how it was identified |
| Budget | Compute, tokens, time, and external work |
| Outcome distribution | All trials rather than selected striking examples |

## Interpretation and failure criteria

A correction to a parser or task specification can alter a conclusion without changing the underlying model. A successful external-memory comparison would support that tested condition, not a general theory of machine cognition. The proposed comparisons are unrun.

**Vocabulary — Held-out evaluation: testing on cases not used to choose the method or its settings.**
